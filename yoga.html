The Yoga Panorama

Learn Yoga from the Foundations to the Wider Tradition

Yoga is much more than physical postures. It includes movement, breath, attention, meditation, ethics, philosophy and self-study.

What Is Yoga?

Yoga Is a Broad Indian Tradition

The word Yoga is linked with ideas of joining, integration and disciplined practice.

Yoga developed through many Indian traditions. It has never been only one method.

Different schools use different practices. Their goals and explanations can also differ.

Yoga Foundations

Begin with the Core Practices

Asana

Asana refers to yoga postures.

Modern yoga uses many forms of movement and posture practice.

Good practice should be gradual and appropriate to the learner.

Pranayama

Pranayama is a traditional field of breath regulation and breath practice.

Different techniques have different methods and purposes.

Breathing practices should be learned carefully.

Meditation

Meditation includes many forms of attention and contemplation.

Traditional Indian systems use different meditation methods.

We will explore these methods in detail on the dedicated Meditation page.

Relaxation

Relaxation practices help learners develop awareness of rest and recovery.

Simple methods can be useful for beginners.

Patanjali Yoga Tradition

The Eight Limbs of Yoga

The Yoga Sutras of Patanjali present an influential eight-part framework.

1. Yama

Ethical principles for our relationship with others.

2. Niyama

Personal observances and disciplines.

3. Asana

A disciplined approach to posture.

4. Pranayama

Regulation and cultivation of the breath.

5. Pratyahara

Turning attention inward and reducing dependence on external sensory objects.

6. Dharana

Developing focused attention.

7. Dhyana

Sustained meditative awareness.

8. Samadhi

A profound state of meditative absorption described in the classical tradition.

Ancient Indian Yoga Panorama

Explore the Many Dimensions of Yoga

Yoga has many streams, texts and teaching traditions. Understanding this diversity is an important part of serious study.

Vedic and Early Indian Thought

Early Indian literature contains ideas that became important to later Yoga traditions.

Upanishadic Traditions

The Upanishads explore the self, reality, knowledge, meditation and liberation.

Bhagavad Gita

The Bhagavad Gita presents several important approaches to Yoga.

These include Karma Yoga, Jnana Yoga and Bhakti Yoga.

Yoga Sutras

Patanjali's Yoga Sutras are one of the best-known classical sources for Yoga study.

Hatha Yoga

Hatha Yoga developed rich traditions of posture, breath, mudra, bandha and contemplative practice.

Raja Yoga

Raja Yoga is commonly associated with disciplined mental and meditative practice.

Jnana Yoga

Jnana Yoga emphasises knowledge, inquiry and discrimination.

Bhakti Yoga

Bhakti Yoga emphasises devotion and a loving relationship with the divine.

Karma Yoga

Karma Yoga explores disciplined action and freedom from attachment to results.

Mantra Traditions

Mantra practices use sacred sounds, words or phrases within particular traditions.

Mudras and Bandhas

These are important concepts in several later Hatha Yoga traditions.

Chakras and Subtle-Body Traditions

Some Yoga and Tantra traditions describe subtle centres and channels.

These belong to traditional frameworks and should not automatically be presented as modern medical anatomy.

Yoga Beyond the Mat

Yoga and Daily Life

Yoga traditions also discuss how we live, think, act and relate to the world.

Ethics

Yoga traditions include teachings about conduct, discipline and responsible living.

Self-Study

Self-observation and study are important themes in several Yoga traditions.

Attention

Yoga develops ways of observing and training attention.

Balance

A thoughtful practice can become part of a balanced daily routine.

Yoga for All Ages

Choose the Right Starting Point

Beginners

Start slowly. Learn the foundations.

Children

Use playful and age-appropriate approaches.

Young People

Build movement, breathing and attention skills.

Adults

Develop a balanced and progressive practice.

Seniors

Choose gentle and adaptable practices where appropriate.

Yoga Teachers

Continue learning beyond basic demonstrations.

Learn Carefully. Practise Responsibly.

Yoga practices differ in difficulty and intensity. A technique suitable for one person may not suit another person.

Beginners should progress gradually. When appropriate, learn directly from a qualified teacher.
