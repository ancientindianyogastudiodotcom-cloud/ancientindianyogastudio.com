Meditation & Inner Practice

Learn to Observe, Focus and Be Present

Meditation is a broad field of practice. Indian traditions developed many ways to train attention, concentration, awareness and contemplation.

Begin Simply Explore Traditions

Understanding Meditation

Meditation Is More Than "Emptying the Mind"

Meditation does not have only one method.

Different traditions use different objects, techniques and goals.

Some practices use the breath. Some use a mantra. Some use visualisation. Some use direct observation.

The common theme is trained attention.

Core Skills

What Can We Learn Through Meditation?

Attention

Learn to notice where attention goes.

Practise returning attention to a chosen object when it wanders.

Concentration

Concentration means staying with one chosen object or task.

Traditional Yoga calls focused attention Dharana.

Awareness

Awareness means noticing experiences without immediately reacting to them.

Observation

Meditation can train us to observe thoughts, feelings, sensations and habits.

Classical Yoga

Dharana, Dhyana and Samadhi

In the eight-limbed framework associated with Patanjali, concentration, meditation and meditative absorption are described as distinct stages.

Dharana

Dharana is focused attention.

The mind is trained to remain with a chosen focus.

Dhyana

Dhyana describes sustained meditation.

Attention becomes more continuous.

Samadhi

Samadhi refers to profound meditative absorption in the classical framework.

Different Indian traditions explain this concept in different ways.

Indian Meditation Panorama

Many Traditions, Many Methods

Indian contemplative traditions are diverse. They should not be treated as one single system.

Yogic Meditation

Yoga traditions contain many methods for developing concentration and meditative awareness.

Vedantic Contemplation

Vedanta includes traditions of inquiry into the self, reality and knowledge.

Buddhist Meditation

Buddhist traditions developed many forms of meditation and contemplative practice.

These traditions have their own teachings, terminology and goals.

Jain Meditation

Jain traditions also contain important practices of contemplation and mental discipline.

Mantra Practice

Some traditions use repeated sacred sounds or phrases as a meditation focus.

Visualisation

Some traditions use carefully defined mental images and visualisation practices.

Beginner Practice

A Simple Starting Exercise

Beginners do not need complicated techniques.

Step 1

Sit comfortably.

Keep the body relaxed.

Step 2

Notice the natural breath.

Do not force it.

Step 3

Notice when attention wanders.

Step 4

Gently return attention to the breath.

Step 5

Continue for a comfortable period.

Regularity matters more than forcing a long session.

Objects of Attention

Different Ways to Focus

Breath

Notice the natural movement of breathing.

Mantra

Use a traditional sound or phrase when appropriate to the method.

Body Awareness

Notice physical sensations with calm attention.

Visual Object

Some meditation traditions use a visual focus.

Sound

Sound can become an object of focused attention.

Inquiry

Some traditions use questions and contemplative inquiry.

Meditation for Different Learners

One Principle Does Not Fit Everyone

Children

Use short, playful attention activities.

Keep instructions simple.

Young People

Short practices can introduce attention and awareness.

Adults

Adults can explore structured concentration and meditation practices.

Seniors

Meditation can be adapted to a comfortable seated position.

Teachers

Teachers need to understand the method before attempting to guide others.

Modern Learning

Meditation in the Digital Age

Technology can make learning easier. It should not replace understanding.

Guided Sessions

Future audio and video resources can guide learners through suitable practices.

Timers

Simple digital timers can support regular practice.

Learning Notes

Digital notes can help learners record questions and observations.

Accessible Learning

Audio, text, visual explanations and captions can support different learners.

Practise Gently and Responsibly

Meditation can sometimes bring difficult thoughts, emotions or memories into awareness.

There is no need to force a practice. Stop if a practice feels overwhelming.

People with specific health or mental-health concerns should seek appropriate professional guidance before choosing practices that may affect them.

Information on this website is educational. It is not medical or psychological treatment.
